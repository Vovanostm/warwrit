## Findings

1. **Every side quest must be memorable**, mainly through one strong character.
2. **Pitch is two or three sentences.** If the quest cannot be told that short,
   it is not ready.
3. **Small job → darker truth.** A routine task turns through one twist;
   tension rises, then a quiet moment lets the player feel the consequence.
4. **No shopping lists.** «Bring 10 pelts» with no story is the anti-pattern;
   clue-following investigation is not.
5. **Grey choices.** No good/evil buttons; bad things can happen to good people.
6. **Consequences arrive later**, so reloading does not tempt; the player
   learns the result from the world, not from a summary.
7. **Dialogue is staged drama**: short replies, subtext, irony, a human
   reaction instead of an explanation.
8. **Quests are anchored in a place**; characters embody their place's need.
9. **Monsters carry human meaning**: the creature says something about people.
10. **Humour sits next to the grim**: dry irony keeps the darkness readable.

## What this changes for Warwrit

- The skill gets a fixed quest spec: pitch → board line → issuer scene →
  clues (obvious, ambiguous, turning) → twist → grey choice → staged outcome
  → delayed consequence.
- A quality rubric replaces "looks fine"; owner remarks of 2026-11-13 become
  regression examples.
- Small catalogue quests keep the same skeleton in miniature: one human
  detail, one light beat, an outcome scene.
